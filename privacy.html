Privacy policy

AddTxt works on your phone. Its developer runs no servers, has no way to see your pictures, and collects nothing about you. This page sets out the few times anything leaves the phone.

Last updated 17 November 2026. This policy covers the AddTxt app for Android and this website.

The short version

  • Your photos, projects, brand kits and settings stay on your phone. They are never uploaded.
  • There are no accounts, no adverts and no analytics in AddTxt.
  • AddTxt itself goes online for two things: to fetch a font you pick, and to look for a newer version.
  • One optional feature, the automatic cut-out, is made by Google and reports some details to Google. It is off until you switch it on.

What stays on your phone

AddTxt keeps a working copy of each photo you open, your projects, your saved stickers, your brand kits with their logos, and your settings in its own private storage on the phone. Removing the app removes them. Finished pictures are saved to the AddTxt album in Pictures, and PDFs to Documents, AddTxt, where your gallery and other apps can see them like any other file.

AddTxt does not ask for access to your gallery, camera, microphone, contacts or location. It sees a photo only when you pick it or share it to AddTxt.

When AddTxt goes online

Fonts

170 fonts are inside the app. When you pick one of the others for the first time, AddTxt downloads that font file from Google Fonts and keeps it on the phone. Google receives the request, which includes your internet address and the name of the font, as it would for any web page that uses Google Fonts. No picture, text or project is sent. Google's note on Google Fonts and privacy has the detail.

New versions

Once a day, when you open AddTxt, it asks GitHub for the number of the latest version. GitHub receives your internet address, as it does for any request, and nothing else from AddTxt. You can switch this off in Settings, under New versions. GitHub's privacy statement covers what GitHub does with requests.

The optional automatic cut-out

"Cut out the subject" and "Lift subject" use Google's ML Kit subject segmentation. It is switched off when AddTxt is installed, and no part of it runs until you switch it on, in Settings or when the app asks the first time you tap one of those buttons.

When it is on:

  • The work is done on your phone. Your pictures are not sent to Google or to anyone else.
  • Google Play services downloads the tool once, which needs the internet.
  • ML Kit sends Google information about the phone (such as its make, model and Android version), about the app, and about how the tool performed, for diagnostics and usage analytics. Google lists this in its ML Kit data disclosure, and handles it under the Google Privacy Policy.

Switch it off again in Settings at any time. "Remove a colour" and "Erase" do the same kind of job by hand and never involve Google.

What is in a saved picture

A photo from a phone camera usually carries the date it was taken and, if you allow it, the place. By default AddTxt keeps these on the pictures it saves, so that they sort beside the original. Switch off "Keep photo details" in Settings to leave them out of everything you save. It is worth doing before sharing a picture outside your team.

This website

This site sets no cookies and has no analytics, adverts or tracking. Its fonts are served from the site itself. It is hosted on GitHub Pages, so GitHub receives your internet address when you load a page, and the download comes from GitHub Releases.

Your rights

The developer holds no personal data about you, so there is nothing to ask for, correct or delete. Under UK data protection law you can still ask, and you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office. For what Google or GitHub hold, use the links above.

AddTxt is not aimed at children and collects nothing from anyone.

Contact

For a question about privacy, open an issue on GitHub. Please do not put personal details in a public issue. If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it and the change is listed in the release notes.
